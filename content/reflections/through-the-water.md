The Bible keeps bringing God's people through water. Noah through the flood. Israel through the sea. A new generation through the Jordan. Each time they go in on one side as one kind of people and come out the other side into a new life.

Baptism is where that pattern lands. This reflection follows the water from the first page to the last.

## The water before

On the first day, "darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters" (Genesis 1:2). On the third, God says, "Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear" (1:9). Life comes up out of the water.

In Noah's day the waters come back over a world gone wrong, and "Noah only remained alive, and they that were with him in the ark" (Genesis 7:23). Peter later says this was a picture: "eight souls were saved by water. The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ" (1 Peter 3:20–21).

(my reading) Peter is careful. The water itself doesn't wash away sin. What saves is what the water stands for: a conscience answering God, made possible by the resurrection.

## Through the sea

At the Red Sea, Israel is trapped between Pharaoh's army and the water. Then "the children of Israel went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground" (Exodus 14:22). They go in as slaves and come out free, and the power that held them is left behind in the water.

Paul calls this a baptism: "all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea; And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea" (1 Corinthians 10:1–2).

Forty years later, the next generation crosses the Jordan into the promised land, and "all the Israelites passed over on dry ground" (Joshua 3:17). Through the sea out of slavery, through the river into the inheritance.

## Washed to come near

Under the law, water also meant getting clean enough to come near God. Before Aaron and his sons served, Moses was told to bring them "unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and shalt wash them with water" (Exodus 29:4).

And water could make an outsider clean. Naaman, a Syrian army commander with leprosy, is told to wash in the Jordan. He's offended, but he goes. He "dipped himself seven times in Jordan... and his flesh came again like unto the flesh of a little child, and he was clean" (2 Kings 5:14).

Then the prophets promise a washing that reaches the heart: "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean... A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you" (Ezekiel 36:25–26). And to Jerusalem, his unfaithful bride, God says, "Then washed I thee with water" (Ezekiel 16:9).

## John at the Jordan

When John the Baptist appears, he goes back to the river Israel crossed to enter the land. He preaches "the baptism of repentance for the remission of sins" (Mark 1:4), and people come "confessing their sins" (Matthew 3:6).

Then Jesus comes to be baptized. John tries to stop him: "I have need to be baptized of thee, and comest thou to me?" (Matthew 3:14). Jesus answers, "Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness" (3:15).

(my reading) He had nothing to confess. He went down into the water anyway, standing in line with the people he came to save, taking their place from the very start.

And when he comes up, all three are there. "The heavens were opened unto him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove... And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased" (Matthew 3:16–17). Over the waters again, as on the first day.

## His other baptism

Jesus uses the word for his death too. "I have a baptism to be baptized with; and how am I straitened till it be accomplished!" (Luke 12:50). He asks James and John, "can ye drink of the cup that I drink of? and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with?" (Mark 10:38).

(my reading) At the Jordan he went into the water. At the cross he went into death, the deeper flood. When a soldier pierced his side, "forthwith came there out blood and water" (John 19:34).

## What baptism means

After the resurrection, Jesus sends his disciples out: "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 28:19). And they do, right away. At Pentecost Peter says, "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost" (Acts 2:38), and "they that gladly received his word were baptized" (2:41).

The New Testament explains what's happening in the water in three pictures.

**A burial and a rising.** "Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death? Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life" (Romans 6:3–4). Going under is a funeral. Coming up is a resurrection. It's the Red Sea again: the old master stays in the water.

**New clothes.** "For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ" (Galatians 3:27). (my reading) That's the answer to the fig leaves of Genesis 3. The person who comes up out of the water is dressed in someone else's righteousness.

**A bride washed.** Christ gave himself for the church "That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church" (Ephesians 5:26–27). It's Ezekiel 16:9 fulfilled: the husband washing his bride.

## Faith first

The pattern in Acts is the same every time. People hear, believe, and are baptized, usually the same day. The Ethiopian official hears Philip explain Isaiah, sees water by the road, and asks, "See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized?" (Acts 8:36). "They went down both into the water, both Philip and the eunuch; and he baptized him" (8:38), and he "went on his way rejoicing" (8:39). The jailer at Philippi believes in the middle of the night "and was baptized, he and all his, straightway" (16:33).

The text also shows that the water isn't what saves on its own. The thief on the cross was never baptized, and Jesus told him, "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise" (Luke 23:43). At Cornelius's house the Holy Ghost fell on the listeners before anyone was baptized, and Peter asked, "Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we?" (Acts 10:47).

(my reading) Baptism follows faith and never replaces it. But in the New Testament it's never treated as optional either. It's how faith goes public: the first thing a new believer does.

**Honest note:** the text holds two things together here. Some verses tie baptism closely to forgiveness: "be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord" (Acts 22:16). Others show forgiveness and the Spirit given before any water. The New Testament doesn't sort that out in a single sentence. Both kinds of verse stand, and this reflection doesn't pretend otherwise.

## The water at the end

In the new creation, "there was no more sea" (Revelation 21:1). (my reading) The water that once meant chaos, flood, and drowning armies is gone. What's left is the water that gives life: "a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb" (22:1). And the last invitation in the Bible is about water: "let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely" (22:17).

## What this means

(my reading) From Genesis to Revelation, God brings his people through water: out of the old world, out of slavery, into the land, clean enough to come near. Jesus went down into the Jordan for us and then into death for us. Baptism is where a person goes in after him. They're buried with him, raised with him, clothed in him, and washed as his bride.

The flood came up over a world that had turned from God. The water of life flows out from his throne. In between, at a river in Judea, God's Son stepped in first.

## Outside: notes from the Greek

*This section comes from outside the KJV text.*

- **"Baptize"** is the Greek *baptizō*, "to dip, plunge, or wash." The KJV mostly keeps the Greek word rather than translating it. In many manuscripts, Mark 7:4 uses the same verb for ordinary washing before a meal ("except they wash, they eat not").
- **"Figure" (1 Peter 3:21)** is *antitypon*, the word behind "antitype": the real thing that an earlier picture pointed to. The flood was the type, and baptism is its fulfillment.
- **"Washing of water by the word" (Ephesians 5:26)** uses *loutron*, a bath. The same word appears in Titus 3:5, "the washing of regeneration."
